## 1. Project Context

### 1.1 Current Situation
At INSPYR, gaps in a new hire's data are found only after the onboarding team already has the case. The current state of the process is:
- JobDiva start notes are optional and often arrive blank.
- Work arrangement is asked in three places and gives contradictory values.
- Paperwork depends on tacit knowledge (FLSA, state, city, client).
- Cases are created by hand and assigned by flagging emails by division.
- Equipment is requested from Chelsea Tech outside the flow.
- The payroll report is built manually and reconciled with a Google Doc.
- Process knowledge is held by a few people.
- Document onboarding takes about 3 days.

### 1.2 Business Problem
Onboarding teams spend time chasing missing or inconsistent data from recruiters, picking paperwork from tacit rules and rebuilding information by hand. The result is:
- slow document onboarding (about 3 days);
- wrong or missing forms;
- missed overhead and bill-back;
- payroll returns;
- dependence on a few people's knowledge.

### 1.3 Why Now
The hackathon has a fixed final submission on October 22, 2026, about eight days of build time after the October 14 checkpoint. October 16 is the cutoff for the foundations (Velocity 2.0, the database and the mocks). If they are not ready, the extensions are dropped and all effort goes to the two Must journeys, J1 and J2.

## 2. Value Proposition

### 2.1 Proposed Solution
The Onboarding Digital Coworker is a digital teammate that takes a new hire's start from the JobDiva start email or a chat conversation. It asks adaptive questions, checks that the data is complete and consistent, and flags exactly which field is missing or contradictory. It then recommends the onboarding path, the paperwork (citing the business rule that applies) and the onboarding owner. It prepares an approval package in which every data point carries a label and its source, and a human approves, returns or rejects it. Only after approval does the Coworker carry out actions, and in this hackathon those actions run against simulated systems only. The Onboarding Team can also ask the Coworker for case status in chat.

**Platform and channel.** The Coworker runs on Velocity 2.0. To users it is one Coworker, reached through a local web app with five views: chat, simulated email inbox, approval, simulated alerts and status. The web app is the only mandatory channel, because Velocity 2.0 has no chat interface of its own.

**How the work is organized.** Inside, an Onboarding Orchestrator owns each case: it keeps the case state, decides which agent acts, manages timing and escalations, builds the package and enforces the approval gate. It routes work to specialized agents that share one context:

| Priority | Agent | What it handles |
|----------|-------|-----------------|
| Must | Intake and Validation | Intake from email or chat, adaptive questions, completeness and consistency check |
| Must | Policy and Case Setup | Path and paperwork recommendation, onboarding owner recommendation |
| Must | JobDiva and Workflow | Simulated actions after approval, payroll package; the only agent that writes to systems |
| Should | Access and Equipment | Email, Teams and app access, laptop, overhead and bill-back |
| Should | Candidate Follow-up | Candidate checklist, tiered reminders, delivered-document check |
| Should | Onboarding Knowledge | Knowledge base with human validation |
| Could | Culture and Belonging (IGS) | IGS handoff, leader feedback and congratulation, new-hire information package |

Notifications (email and Teams) are a shared tool with templates per audience, not an agent. The demo shows at least three specialized agents (Intake and Validation, Policy and Case Setup, JobDiva and Workflow) collaborating on one case under the orchestrator.

**Case lifecycle.** Received → Validated (or Needs clarification, back to Validated when answered) → Case set up → Ready for review → Approval gate → Approved, Rejected, or returned for correction (back to Validated) → Actions executed → Documents pending, In verification, Sent to payroll (Should) → Handoff to IGS (Could) → Closed. A duplicate (same person and start date) is blocked at Received. Every agent action is written to the audit log with the agent, case, date, and the data and rules used.

### 2.2 Current Alternative
The recruiter submits the start in JobDiva and an automatic email goes out. The onboarding team builds the case by hand, chases gaps with the recruiter and picks the paperwork from tacit knowledge. The candidate signs and onboarding reviews. Payroll builds a manual report. The recruiter separately requests the laptop from Chelsea Tech.

## 4. Business Context & Scope

**In Scope:**
- Intake by email and chat with adaptive questions.
- Validation of the 7 recruiter-note fields and the consistency rules. The fields are: work city and state (where the consultant works), FLSA, billing contact, timesheet approver, equipment (hardware tier and provider), sponsorship and visa, and additional notes (optional). The mandatory JobDiva and chat data are also checked: role, manager, start date, client, division, line of business, employment type and vendor company, work arrangement, and pay and bill rates.
- Business rules that determine the paperwork (contract type, FLSA, state, city and client), the onboarding owner (client type and role) and the welcome kit (contract type).
- Access, equipment, overhead and bill-back (Should).
- Approval package and human approval gate.
- Simulated JobDiva, IT, equipment, payroll and email actions.
- Follow-up with a synthetic candidate (Should).
- Knowledge base with human validation (Should).
- Handoff to IGS with accelerated timing (Could).

**Out of Scope:**
- Recruiting inside JobDiva before the notification.
- Legal classification or compensation decisions.
- Background checks, drug screens, I-9 and E-Verify.
- Real equipment purchases or provisioning.
- Payroll processes after the handoff.
- Production JobDiva API and real data.
- Real e-signature and legal validation of documents.
- Consolidation of the company-wide talent database.
- Production rollout across all locations.
- The Coworker never writes to the real JobDiva.

**Won't v1:**
- The pilot (4 to 6 weeks: one client or contract type, real onboarding mailbox, real JobDiva integration).
- The scale-up (all clients, full document verification, full IGS flow, analytics).
- Microsoft Teams as a channel is optional and only added after the demo is stable.
- Should and Could items are built only if the Must journeys are stable.

## 5. Constraints & Assumptions

### Constraints
| CONST ID | Type | Constraint |
|----------|------|-----------|
| CONST-001 | Schedule | Final submission on October 22, 2026, with October 16 as the cutoff for the foundations |
| CONST-002 | Budget | Model usage must stay within the assigned budget and be logged per run |
| CONST-003 | Data / Security | Synthetic data only, with no production connections and no credentials in the repository |
| CONST-004 | Legal | No legal decisions by the Coworker; FLSA, classification and compensation belong to the field or HR |
| CONST-005 | Platform access | Nobody on the team can register a bot in the INSPYR tenant, so Microsoft Teams is optional |
| CONST-006 | Platform | Velocity 2.0 is the mandatory platform (agent runtime, orchestration and models). The demo runs locally on simulated services, through a local web app |

### Assumptions
| ASM ID | Assumption | Risk If Wrong |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| ASM-001 | Velocity 2.0 licenses and JobDiva API credentials are pending confirmation as of October 16 | Without Velocity 2.0 licenses the build is blocked. Without JobDiva credentials the demo runs on the JobDiva mock only, which is the default mode |
| ASM-002 | Recruiter emails have no standard format, so variations are tolerated and confirmation is asked | Intake could misread start data or ask too many confirmations, slowing recruiters and reducing trust |
| ASM-003 | The rules catalog (forms by state and city, overhead, bill-back) is seed data still being validated with Tonya Ramirez's team | Paperwork, overhead or bill-back recommendations could be wrong or incomplete and need rework |
| ASM-004 | Should and Could items are built only if the Must journeys are stable | If the Must journeys are not stable, the extensions are dropped and the delivered value is limited to J1 and J2 |

### Key Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Eight days to build and test | Build J1 and J2 end to end first; Should and Could only with a stable demo |
| Velocity 2.0 learning curve or licenses | Confirm licenses; a minimal case working on day one |
| Tacit business rules (forms, overhead, bill-back) | Validate the rules catalog with Tonya Ramirez's team; cite rule IDs |
| Recruiter emails with no standard format | Tolerate variations and ask for confirmation; propose a template |
| Extraction errors affecting payroll or classification | Mandatory human validation; no autonomous decisions |
| Exposure of visa, sponsorship or FLSA | Role-based permissions and data minimization in messages |
| Too many reminders to candidates or leaders | Configurable cadence and a send limit |
| Model cost overrun | Small models for extraction; usage logged per run |
| Low adoption by the teams | Co-design with onboarding and LDC |
| Demo failure on presentation day | Preloaded scenarios and a backup video |
| JobDiva API credentials not available in time | JobDiva mock as the default mode |
