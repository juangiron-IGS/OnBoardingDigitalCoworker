## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This test plan defines how the Onboarding Digital Coworker will be verified against the functional requirements in FRS-001 and the non-functional targets in the NFR Register. The system is a local, single-machine demo with synthetic data and simulated enterprise systems. Velocity 2.0 is the delivery method that produced the project documents, not an agent runtime (SAD ADR-006), and agents reach a model only through `agents/common/model_client.py`. Verification runs as one test cycle before the fixed submission and presentation on 2026-10-28.

### 1.2 Scope

> **Source:** `scope.list` (Test Scope) records what QA will and will not test, with the rationale for each exclusion. It is cited here, not restated.

At a glance, Must scope is journeys J1 and J2, the Onboarding Orchestrator, the three Must specialized agents, the approval gate, the audit log, the JobDiva connectors and mocks, and the negative governance tests. The Should agents and the Could features that were built after version 1.3 are tested too (Test Cases §21B and §21D to §21K). The following are deliberately excluded (`scope.list`, out-of-scope entries 2 and 3):

1. **Culture and Belonging (IGS), leader feedback, status dashboard and new-hire package (the Could stories)** were excluded in versions 1.0 to 1.4 because they were Could priority and time was short. They are built since version 1.5 and the Test Cases cover them (§21H and §21I). `scope.list` in the QSOT still records the exclusion, so Open Item 8 asks the Product Owner and Development to confirm the scope change.
2. **Real systems, real orders and production access**, because the project is synthetic and simulated only (TC-002, TC-003, NFR-COMP-001).
3. **FLSA, classification, compensation and legal decisions.** The system escalates these and never decides them (BR-001), so the test covers the escalation, not the decision.
4. **Security testing (the four cases of module SEC)**, because the application runs only on localhost with synthetic data (decision of 2026-10-27). The secret scan, the token-in-logs check, the startup-per-mode check, the TLS inspection and the role-selector check are not run as exit criteria. The governance tests stay in scope: approval before any action, the single writer, no legal decisions and the audit log are rules of the project, not security controls.

The business scenarios agreed with the onboarding team (the demo scenario and acceptance criteria AC1 to AC12 of the checkpoint) are part of the scope as a traceability layer: the Test Cases document maps each of them to cases (Test Cases §23.5 and §23.6) and lists the product gaps they expose (§23.7). AC4, AC10 and AC11 are Should and their agents are built, so their cases are no longer conditional. AC12 is Could and is covered by the IGS and feedback cases since version 1.5.

The JobDiva sandbox is a declared risk, not coverage. No sandbox credentials are confirmed, so sandbox mode is exercised only through negative and startup tests (see TR-001).

## 2. Test Strategy

### 2.1 Testing Levels

> **Source:** `levels.list` (Testing Levels & Ownership) records every level this project needs, its owner, and whether it is automated or manual. It also carries the developer-level baseline QUAL-001 to QUAL-008, which it cites from the shared testing information file. None of this is restated or re-decided here.

**Ownership boundary.** This project has no dedicated QA, so the development team owns every level in `levels.list`. The Product Owner accepts coverage and is the proposed QA lead / coverage acceptor (`team.list`). Two kinds of content are kept apart:

- **The developer-level baseline** (test types, pytest-based tooling, mocking rules, coverage target, CI gate, QUAL-001 to QUAL-008) is Development's decision. This plan cites it and never re-targets it.
- **The Quality-phase elaboration** is this plan's own work: per-level approach, design techniques, cycle, evidence and traceability.

The QUAL items are assumed defaults pending Development's confirmation (`levels.list`). They are not settled facts, and any change by Development is a change request, not an edit made here (see TR-007).

### 2.2 Test Types and Approach

The levels below are exactly those `levels.list` records as applying. The test case catalog (`3-test-cases`) is built from them.

#### Unit
- **Targets:** rule lookups and case-state transitions (FR-CASE-001, FR-CASE-002, FR-REC-005, BR-006).
- **Approach:** white-box tests with pytest, `pytest-cov` and `unittest.mock`. Mocks sit only at connector boundaries (MCP servers and the model client), never around case service or approval gate logic (QUAL-007).
- **Focus:** the case-state machine (`tests/unit/test_case_states.py`, 7 tests), the rule catalog lookups (`tests/unit/test_rule_lookup.py`), the package rules (`tests/unit/test_package.py`) and the model client with its usage cap (`tests/unit/test_model_client.py`). These tests exist and are reused, not duplicated.
- **Automation:** automated (QUAL-002, QUAL-005).

#### Integration (connector tests in mock mode)
- **Targets:** the seven connectors behind tool/MCP interfaces: JobDiva, Outlook mailbox, Teams, Chelsea Tech, Paychex, LDC tracker, knowledge base (INT-001 to INT-007, NFR-COMPAT-002, INF-003, TINT-001).
- **Approach:** contract-style tests that drive each connector through its interface in mock mode. They check that the interface is the same across modes, with no agent change when a mock is replaced. The JobDiva READ server (mock and read-only sandbox) and the MCP transport already have tests in `tests/connectors/`.
- **Focus:** the JobDiva connector, where duplicate search and the mock/sandbox mode boundary carry the most risk. The mode loader, the sandbox write refusal and the token-in-logs check are tested with a mocked HTTP transport. A run against the real JobDiva sandbox is not possible without credentials (TR-001).
- **Automation:** automated (QUAL-003).

#### System / end-to-end
- **Targets:** J1 (FR-ITK, FR-ADQ, FR-VAL, FR-AUD-001, FR-AUD-003) and J2 (FR-REC, FR-OWN, FR-PKG, FR-GATE, FR-ACT, FR-CASE, FR-AUD-002, FR-STS, FR-WEB, FR-PRV), plus the use cases UC-001 to UC-005.
- **Approach:** end-to-end runs on the seed scenarios through the local web app. Agents run on the deterministic `rules` model engine (`MODEL_PROVIDER=rules`, SAD ADR-006), so the runs need no model key and are repeatable. The agents call the systems over MCP by default, with an in-process fallback (`AGENT_TRANSPORT`).
- **Focus:** at least three agents collaborating on one case (Intake and Validation, Policy and Case Setup, JobDiva and Workflow under the orchestrator), and the demo scenario of the onboarding team (W-2 in Philadelphia, two gaps, recommendation, approval, actions and duplicate), which the Test Cases document verifies in QC-CASE-P-003.
- **Automation:** automated (QUAL-001).
- **Verification Methods discharged:** NFR-SEC-001, NFR-SEC-004 (log completeness against the lifecycle), NFR-USE-003, NFR-REL-001, NFR-REL-002, NFR-REL-003, NFR-REL-005, NFR-REL-004 (with Should agents disabled via the FEATURE_* flags) and NFR-PERF-004 (reconciliation of logged runs).

#### Negative governance tests (one per inviolable rule)
- **Targets:** BR-001, BR-002, BR-003, BR-004, BR-011, BR-014 and the NFR-SEC and NFR-REL controls that protect them. `levels.list` lists the required negative tests. This plan adds no rule to that list and drops none.
- **Approach:** each test tries to violate one rule and asserts refusal plus an audit entry. They are black-box where the UI is the entry point and API-level where the connector or database role is the boundary.
- **Focus:** the JobDiva write path (the sandbox write refusal and the check that no write call reaches JobDiva in either mode), the startup test per `JOBDIVA_MODE`, the token-never-logged check, the `audit_writer` UPDATE/DELETE refusal, and the role-by-field visibility of visa, sponsorship and FLSA data.
- **Automation:** automated, 100% must pass, and any failure blocks merge (QUAL-004, QUAL-008).
- **Verification Methods discharged:** NFR-SEC-002, NFR-SEC-003, NFR-SEC-004 (edit/delete refusal), NFR-SEC-005 (token in logs), NFR-SEC-007, NFR-REL-006, NFR-COMP-001 (production-URL negative test), NFR-COMP-002 (one negative test per human decision, and audience-template tests) and NFR-PERF-004 (usage cap refusal, TC-004).

#### Regression and smoke
- **Targets:** the whole automated suite on every pull request. The smoke test is `tests/test_smoke.py`.
- **Approach:** run the full suite and `ruff check` for every change. Until `.github/workflows/ci.yml` exists, the gate is run locally (see TR-003).
- **Automation:** automated (QUAL-008).
- **Verification Methods discharged:** the secret scan of NFR-SEC-005, and the fixed sample-email extraction set of NFR-MAINT-003 (OPS-004).

#### Performance (scripted)
- **Targets:** NFR-PERF-001 (package Ready within 5 minutes), NFR-PERF-002 (views within 2 seconds and chat receipt indicator within 1 second) and NFR-PERF-003 (at least 10 simultaneously open cases, isolated by case ID).
- **Approach:** a Python timing harness (`time.perf_counter` and audit-log timestamps) runs every seed scenario for NFR-PERF-001. A Playwright navigation-timing script covers the five views for NFR-PERF-002. For NFR-PERF-003, at least 10 cases are open at once and J1 and J2 run concurrently while isolation is checked in the audit log.
- **Focus:** NFR-PERF-001 is an upper limit for every case, not an average. With the `rules` engine the measured times contain no model latency, so they are a lower bound for a real model engine (see TR-002).
- **Automation:** scripted. No load-testing tool is used because the demo is single-user on localhost.

#### Security and other NFR checks (per each NFR's own Verification Method)
These follow the NFR Register's `Verification Method` exactly, with no substitution. They are assigned to levels as follows:

| NFR | Verification Method (from the NFR Register) | Implemented in |
|-----|---------------------------------------------|----------------|
| NFR-SEC-006 | Inspect configuration of each outbound connection | Manual configuration audit, recorded as evidence |
| NFR-AVAIL-001 | Rehearsal log of at least 3 consecutive error-free J1 and J2 runs, plus the reset-time check | Manual rehearsal on the presentation machine |
| NFR-AVAIL-002 | Run the reset command and compare with the seed definitions. Review the backup video against the J1 and J2 steps | Scripted state comparison plus manual video review |
| NFR-USE-001 | Scripted walkthrough per role, plus the reviewer score | System level plus User acceptance |
| NFR-USE-002 | Manual keyboard walkthrough and an automated contrast check on all five views | Manual walkthrough. The contrast check runs inside the Playwright script already named above |
| NFR-MAINT-001, NFR-MAINT-002 | Architecture review of the component catalog and its consumers. For MAINT-001, replace a mock with a stub and run J1 and J2 | Manual review. The stub replacement runs at the Integration level |
| NFR-MAINT-003 | Fault-injection drills (failing step identified in under 5 minutes) | Manual timed drill |
| NFR-MAINT-004 | Change-and-rerun tests for rules, templates and mode, plus a configuration-strategy review | Integration and System levels, plus manual review |
| NFR-COMPAT-001 | Five-view walkthrough on Chrome and Edge | Manual |
| NFR-COMPAT-002 | Connector tests in mock mode and an interface-conformance review | Integration level, plus manual review |
| NFR-COMPAT-003 | Clean-checkout reproduction by a team member other than the author, using only the README | Manual |

#### User acceptance
- **Targets:** the demonstration of J1 and J2 to the business reviewer, scored 4 out of 5 or higher (NFR-USE-001).
- **Approach:** manual demonstration on the presentation machine. The Product Owner accepts coverage.
- **Automation:** manual, as `levels.list` records. There is no separate UAT document in this phase (see Section 5).

### 3.3 Test Data Management

> **Source:** `data.list` (Test Data Management) records the data source, privacy rules, volume and ownership. The compliance basis is NFR-COMP-001. Both are cited here, not restated.

Test data is synthetic or anonymized only (TC-003). No masked production copy is used and no production data may be connected, so no unmasked production data is acceptable anywhere in this phase. Restricted fields (visa, sponsorship, FLSA) are synthetic and are logged by reference, never by value (BR-011). The development team creates the seed data and resets it before each cycle. The seed data does not yet hold the scenario of the onboarding team (a W-2 in Philadelphia with the timesheet approver blank and a contradictory work arrangement): seed 02 is a Los Angeles W-2 and seed 04 is a Philadelphia contractor. TD-018 in the Test Cases document defines it, and building it is part of the entry work.

## 10. Requirements Coverage Approach

How coverage will be shown, and what `3-test-cases` must produce:

- Every in-scope FR-XXX (the 50 Must FRs of the FRS) gets at least one positive and one negative test case, or all the acceptance criteria of its Backlog story mapped to cases, plus boundary cases wherever a business rule or use case defines a boundary. "In scope" means every Must FR in FRS-001, plus any Should FR whose agent is built (FEATURE_* flag enabled).
- Every NFR-XXX with a quantified acceptance criterion gets at least one test case measuring against that criterion, using the Verification Method assigned in Section 2.2. An NFR without a quantified criterion is recorded as not testable as specified, not given an invented criterion.
- The Prioritized Backlog's acceptance criteria are the authoritative expected results. Where a story has none, the requirement's own statement or the NFR target is used. Expected results are never invented, and each case records its source.
- Coverage is reported as a two-way traceability matrix in the Test Cases document. It names every uncovered or partially covered in-scope requirement and states its status as "executable today" or "designed, pending code", never claiming full coverage by omission.

**Target:** 100% of in-scope FR-XXX entries and 100% of measurably-specified NFR-XXX entries covered.

**Business traceability.** The scenarios agreed with the onboarding team are traced as well as the requirements: the nine steps of the demo scenario and the acceptance criteria AC1 to AC12 of the checkpoint map to cases in Test Cases §23.5 and §23.6. A step or criterion that the application cannot perform today is reported as such (Test Cases §23.7), not hidden behind a covered requirement.

The Backlog is traced at the level of its acceptance criteria as well: the 28 Must stories carry 72 acceptance criteria, and every one is mapped to a case (Test Cases §23.9). The 12 use cases and 14 business rules of the FRS are mapped in Test Cases §23.10 and §23.11. The repository seed scenarios and the cases that use them are in Test Cases §23.8.

**Excluded from the denominator** (`scope.list`, out-of-scope entries):
- FR-IGS-001, FR-IGS-002, FR-FBK-001, FR-FBK-002, FR-DSH-001, FR-DSH-002, FR-NHP-001 and FR-NHP-002 (Could priority). They are built and covered since version 1.5, but they are reported apart from the Must denominator until the QSOT scope is changed (Open Item 8).
- The Should FRs FR-ACC, FR-CHK, FR-DOC, FR-PAY and FR-KB are built and covered, and are reported apart from the Must denominator as well. FR-ACC-004 has no case.
- Real-system behavior and FLSA, classification, compensation and legal decisions. Only the escalation is tested (BR-001).

NFR-COMP-002 and the role-based checks of FR-PRV-002 remain in scope. They are verified for every audience template that exists, and recorded as "designed, pending code" where a template does not exist yet.
